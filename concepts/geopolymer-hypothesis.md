# Geopolymer Hypothesis (Davidovits)

Joseph Davidovits proposed that the pyramid blocks are not quarried stone but geopolymer concrete — limestone rubble reconstituted with a binder (natron, lime, water) and poured into molds.

## What It Would Explain

- (a) The irregular block shapes (poured into place, not dressed)
- (b) The tight mortar joints (the "mortar" is actually the geopolymer binder)
- (c) The presence of fossil shells in the blocks oriented randomly (unlike natural limestone bedding, which has aligned shell orientation)

## Evidence

The hypothesis is testable: geopolymer concrete has a different microstructure from natural limestone (amorphous vs. crystalline matrix). Barsoum et al. (2006) found that some casing stones contain silica-based microstructures consistent with geopolymer chemistry.

## Status

The mainstream view is that the blocks are natural limestone. The geopolymer hypothesis has not been definitively refuted — it has been ignored. If even some blocks are geopolymer, the construction logistics change dramatically (no quarrying, no transport, no lifting — just pouring).

This is the single most disruptive untested hypothesis in pyramid studies. A systematic petrographic, XRD, and chemical analysis of blocks from different courses and chambers could resolve it. See [[construction-methods]].

## Tests

- Petrographic thin-section analysis of core blocks (amorphous matrix = geopolymer, crystalline = natural)
- XRD analysis for mineral phases characteristic of geopolymer reactions
- Chemical analysis for elevated sodium (from natron) in the matrix
- Comparison with known geopolymer samples made from Egyptian limestone
