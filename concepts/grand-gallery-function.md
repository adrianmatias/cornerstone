# Grand Gallery Function

The Grand Gallery is a 47 m long corbelled vault rising 8.5 m, with an overhanging stepped ceiling. Its function is debated.

## Structural Hypothesis

The Gallery may be designed to relieve load on the King's Chamber below, distributing the enormous compressive force of the 6 million tonnes above. The stepped corbel design would serve this structural purpose.

## Counterweight System Hypothesis

The Gallery's design is consistent with a counterweight rail system:
- Floor has lateral benches (possibly for guide rails)
- Walls are corbelled inward (possibly to control the path of a counterweight trolley)
- Width (~2 m) is sufficient for a sledge-mounted counterweight of several tonnes

If a counterweight in the Grand Gallery was connected via a rope system to an external ramp on the south face, it could have been loaded with rubble or stone, then released to lift granite beams. When the load descended in the Gallery, the granite beam would rise. This would explain how 80-tonne granite beams from [[aswan-quarry]] were raised to 43 m (the King's Chamber level).

This is consistent with Herodotus's description of "machines" — though Herodotus was 2000 years late and unreliable.

## The "Portcullis" System

The blocking stones in the Antechamber (between the Grand Gallery and the King's Chamber) may be related to the counterweight system — either as security devices or as mechanical elements.

## What Remains Unknown

- No direct evidence of counterweight use (no rope marks, no wear patterns documented)
- The Gallery's geometry could serve purely structural purposes
- The [[big-void]] above the Grand Gallery may be related to its function
