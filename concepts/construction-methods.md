# Construction Methods

The Great Pyramid was built by quarrying an estimated 2.3 million large blocks weighing 6 million tonnes total. The majority are local limestone from the Giza Plateau, roughly dressed, irregular in size. Casing stones were fine white Tura limestone. Granite for the King's Chamber came from Aswan (~800 km upriver), with blocks up to 80 tonnes.

## The Throughput Problem

The binding constraint is placement rate: ~1 block every 2-3 minutes during working hours over ~26 years. This assumes 2.3 million blocks, ~26 years of construction, and seasonal work patterns (heavier during Nile inundation when agricultural labor was available). The geometry worsens as the pyramid rises — shorter side lengths mean less workspace, more turning maneuvers, less room for ramps.

## Ramp Theories

Every proposed ramp solution fails on at least one count:

- **Large straight ramp**: ~1.4 million m³ of material, zero archaeological evidence, would bury casing on one side.
- **Spiral external ramp**: Covers casing on all sides, tight turning radii at higher courses.
- **Internal ramp** ([[houdin-internal-ramp]]): Solves volume + casing problems but unproven. The notch at the 3rd course ("Bob" room) is suggestive. Microgravity surveys found density variations consistent but ambiguous. ScanPyramids muon tomography found the [[big-void]] above the Grand Gallery, not an internal ramp spiral.
- **Combined ramp + lever** (consensus position): Ramps for lower courses, levers for top 3-4%. Acknowledged as incomplete — never engineering-validated end to end.

The Bent Pyramid at Dahshur (slope change from 54° to 43°) is evidence that builders discovered steep gradients + shrinking workspace were unsolvable together. See [[pyramid-evolution-timeline]].

## Alternative Construction Methods

- **Block rolling**: Wooden cradles attached to blocks to turn them into cylinders — see [[block-rolling-hypothesis]].
- **Geopolymer concrete** ([[geopolymer-hypothesis]]): Blocks poured rather than quarried. Would explain irregular shapes, tight joints, random fossil shell orientation. Untested on a systematic scale.
- **Grand Gallery counterweight**: See [[grand-gallery-function]].

## Workforce

Not slaves. Workers' cemeteries discovered 1990 by [[lehner]] and Hawass. ~14,500-30,000 total workers (permanent core ~5,000, seasonal corvée). Workers received ~10 loaves of bread and a jug of beer per day. Hierarchically organized: two crews of ~2,000 → gangs of 1,000 → five phyles of 200 → groups of ~20. See [[workforce-organization]].

## Key Evidence

- **Diary of Merer** ([[diary-of-merer]]): Only contemporary textual record. Documents Tura limestone transport in year 27 of Khufu. ~200 blocks/month by boat.
- **Djehutihotep tomb painting**: 172 men pulling a 60-tonne alabaster statue on a sledge, with liquid poured ahead of runners.
- **ScanPyramids** ([[scanpyramids]]): Muon tomography detected the [[big-void]] and [[north-face-corridor]].

## Open Questions

- How were 80-tonne granite beams raised to 43 m? Best model: [[grand-gallery-function]] as counterweight system.
- Was the [[houdin-internal-ramp]] used? Unproven.
- Are some blocks geopolymer? See [[geopolymer-hypothesis]].
- Could block rolling have supplemented dragging? See [[block-rolling-hypothesis]].
