# Purpose and Function

The consensus says "tomb of [[khufu]]." But the evidence inside the Great Pyramid does not cleanly support this interpretation.

## Problems with the Tomb Hypothesis

- No mummy has ever been found in the Great Pyramid.
- The sarcophagus in the King's Chamber is too large to have been placed after the chamber was built (integrated into construction).
- The sarcophagus shows no lid and no signs of having held a body.
- The [[internal-structure]] (Subterranean Chamber, Grand Gallery, multiple shafts) is far more complex than any other Egyptian tomb.
- The Subterranean Chamber was left unfinished — a plan change, or never intended as a burial chamber?
- The Queen's Chamber was sealed off and may never have been intended for burial.

The "funerary" interpretation is a default assumption inherited from the broader pyramid tradition, not a conclusion drawn from evidence found inside this specific pyramid. The mortuary cult that operated in the complex temples for centuries does indicate Khufu was interred somewhere in the complex — but doesn't prove it was in the King's Chamber.

## The "Tomb as Instrument" Model

The Great Pyramid may be simultaneously a tomb, an astronomical instrument, and a ritual structure. The Egyptians did not separate engineering from ritual the way we do. The [[shaft-alignments]] to specific stars suggest the internal geometry was designed to channel starlight — or the concept of starlight — into the chambers, consistent with the Egyptian stellar afterlife theology (the king ascending to circumpolar stars).

The "initiation" interpretation — that the internal chambers were designed for ritual passage (subterranean → ascending → queen's → grand gallery → king's as a symbolic journey through the duat to the stars) — reconciles the "tomb" and "astronomical instrument" models: the tomb IS the instrument.

## Alternative/Complementary Functions

- **Astronomical instrument**: [[shaft-alignments]] encode stellar targets. The pyramid may have been a calibrated astronomical instrument embedded in 2.6 million m³ of stone.
- **Solar function**: The pyramid shape (slanted sides as sun rays, pyramidion as solar symbol) is well-established in Egyptology but usually treated as decorative. The geometry may encode specific solar/stellar alignments.
- **Knowledge preservation**: Medieval Arabic traditions attribute a "library" function to the pyramids. See [[occult-and-hermetic-traditions]].
- **Ritual/initiatory**: The internal passage as a journey of the soul through the duat. See [[occult-and-hermetic-traditions]].

## The Deepest Gap

Our inability to reconcile these functions may reflect our own categories, not theirs. The boundary between engineering and ritual in Old Kingdom Egypt was not the same as ours. The Great Pyramid may have been designed as a machine for the ritual transformation of the king's soul — a funerary monument whose internal geometry encoded the journey of the soul.
