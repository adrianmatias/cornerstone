# Occult and Hermetic Traditions

Medieval and ancient traditions attribute to the Great Pyramid functions beyond burial — knowledge preservation, initiation, and esoteric transformation.

## Medieval Arabic Sources

Al-Maqrizi (1364-1442) and al-Idrisi preserve traditions that the pyramids were built before the biblical flood by a king named Saurid ibn Salhouk, who saw a vision of a flood and built the pyramids to preserve knowledge and treasure. This attributes a "library" or archive function to the pyramids rather than a funerary one.

These are medieval legends, not evidence — but they reflect a persistent intuition that the Great Pyramid is more than a tomb.

## Hermetic Tradition

The Corpus Hermeticum, attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, associates the pyramids with esoteric knowledge and initiation rites. The Hermetic tradition positions the pyramids as sites of spiritual transformation, not merely burial.

## The Initiation Interpretation

The internal chambers may have been designed for ritual passage:
- Subterranean Chamber → Ascending Passage → Queen's Chamber → Grand Gallery → King's Chamber
- This sequence can be read as a symbolic journey through the duat (underworld) to the circumpolar stars

This interpretation has been proposed by Egyptologists (notably [[lehner]] in his earlier work) and is consistent with the [[internal-structure]]. It reconciles the "tomb" and "astronomical instrument" models: the tomb IS the instrument — a machine for the ritual transformation of the king's soul. See [[purpose-and-function]].
