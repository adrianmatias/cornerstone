# The Sphinx and the Giza Complex

The Great Sphinx of Giza (~73 m long, ~20 m high) was carved from a bedrock knoll on the Giza Plateau. The surrounding limestone was cut away on three sides to form the Sphinx enclosure (~73 m × 45 m, walls up to ~8 m deep). Its relationship to the pyramid complex and its dating are among the most controversial topics in Egyptology.

## The Standard Attribution

Conventionally attributed to pharaoh Khafre (4th Dynasty, c. 2558-2532 BC). Based on:
- Proximity to Khafre's pyramid and causeway
- The southern wall of the Sphinx enclosure follows the oblique angle of Khafre's causeway
- The Sphinx Temple was built from blocks quarried directly from the Sphinx enclosure — establishing temple and enclosure-cutting as contemporaneous
- The Sphinx Temple was built on the foundation of the preexisting northern enclosure wall of Khafre's Valley Temple
- The Dream Stele of Thutmose IV (c. 1401 BC) between the paws mentions "...Khaf..." which most Egyptologists read as Khafre (though this is 1,100 years after the supposed construction)
- Luminescence dating of the Sphinx Temple and Valley Temple gave dates in the middle-to-late 3rd millennium BCE

## The Water Erosion Controversy

### Schoch's Hypothesis

Robert M. Schoch (PhD Geology, Yale; Boston University), inspired by R.A. Schwaller de Lubicz and John Anthony West, presented his hypothesis at the Geological Society of America in October 1991:

- The **vertical fissure patterns** on the north and south enclosure walls, and the **rounded, undulating weathering profile** on the lower body, are diagnostic of weathering by **prolonged direct rainfall** on flat-bedded limestone — not wind/sand erosion.
- Vertical channels represent pre-existing joints preferentially **enlarged by water flowing down wall faces** over a sustained period.
- Old Kingdom tombs on the same plateau show only **angular, horizontal wind/sand erosion** — distinctly different patterns.
- **Seismic refraction surveys** (with geophysicist Thomas Dobecki) measured subsurface weathering 2-4+ m deep, with deepest weathering at the front (oldest exposure), progressively shallower toward the rear — requiring far longer than 4,500 years of exposure.
- The head is disproportionately small for the body, suggesting it was **re-carved** from an earlier (possibly leonine) original.
- The climate of Giza has been arid since ~3000 BCE; substantial rainfall ceased by ~5000-7000 BCE. Therefore the Sphinx enclosure must have been carved during a wetter period.

### Geological Evidence Cited

| Feature | Schoch's Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Deep vertical fissures on enclosure walls | Rainfall/runoff erosion widened over sustained period |
| Rounded, undulating surfaces on body and walls | Precipitation-induced weathering on flat-bedded limestone |
| Erosion intensity gradient — western wall most eroded | Rainfall runoff from plateau to the west (paleohydrology) |
| Absence of comparable patterns on Old Kingdom tombs | Age difference, not lithology alone |
| Subsurface weathering 2-4 m+ (seismic refraction) | Requires far longer exposure than 4,500 years |
| Fissures wider at top than bottom | Rainwater running down surfaces, not groundwater seepage |
| Sphinx Temple core blocks weathered under Old Kingdom granite veneer | Temples built around an already-extant, older Sphinx |

David Coxill (1998, geologist) independently observed fissures that do not displace strata (as tectonic fractures would), concluding a prehistoric date.

### Dating Estimates

| Position | Proposed Date | Key Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Mainstream 4th Dynasty | ~2558-2532 BCE (Khafre) | Lehner, Hawass, most Egyptologists |
| Schoch water-erosion (conservative) | 7000-5000 BCE | Schoch, West |
| Schoch (latest) | ~9700 BCE (end of last ice age) | Schoch |
| Reader middle position | ~3000 BCE (Early Dynastic) | Colin Reader |
| Hancock/Bauval | ~10,500 BCE | Hancock, Bauval |
| Manichev/Parkhomenko | Tens of thousands of years | Two Ukrainian geologists (not accepted) |

The "20,000 BC" claim appears in speculative literature relating to the last glacial period when the Sahara was wetter, but is not supported by peer-reviewed geological evidence.

## Mainstream Response

### Mark Lehner (Yale PhD dissertation on the Sphinx, 1992)

- The Sphinx Temple was built from enclosure blocks — establishing contemporaneity. The temple is stratigraphically 4th Dynasty.
- The Sphinx Temple was built **on the foundation of the Valley Temple's preexisting northern enclosure wall** — meaning the Sphinx/temple postdate the Valley Temple.
- The southern enclosure wall follows Khafre's causeway angle — enclosure cut after causeway was laid out.
- Both enclosure and temple left **unfinished** — consistent with premature 4th Dynasty cessation.
- The Sphinx limestone is **highly heterogeneous** — Member II contains shale interbeds, fossil-rich layers, and zones of differing porosity, producing strongly differential weathering under uniform conditions.
- **Groundwater seepage, capillary rise, and salt crystallization (haloclasty)** can produce vertical channel features mimicking rainfall erosion.
- The Sphinx sits in a depression accumulating seasonal runoff, intensifying water-mediated weathering even under modern arid conditions.
- Repeatedly buried in sand for ~4/5 of its known existence; buried portions experienced different (moist sub-sand) conditions.
- Granite casing on Valley Temple applied contemporaneously with limestone core (not later over weathered stone).

### Zahi Hawass

- Strongly rejects any pre-dynastic dating.
- Points to poor quality of Giza limestone as basis for severe erosion.
- Found **unweathered rock at the base of the Sphinx** under large Tura-quality limestone casing blocks — arguing the rough surface was left by original builders.
- No archaeological evidence of civilization before 4000 BCE in the Nile Valley (with Kenneth Feder).

### K. Lal Gauri (University of Louisville geologist)

- **Salt exfoliation from atmospheric condensation** (dew) is the primary weathering mechanism, not rainfall.
- **Haloclasty:** The Sphinx lies above the Nile aquifer; capillary action moves groundwater to the stone surface, where salt crystallizes and breaks down the limestone. Particularly destructive in arid climates with periodic moisture.
- Sharp corners wear faster from salt exfoliation, producing spheroidal weathering pattern Schoch attributes to rain.
- Fissures could have formed from **fluctuations in the underground water table over millions of years** (pre-Sphinx geological features).

### James A. Harrell (University of Toledo)

- Weathering under **wet sand** during burial periods: rainfall and occasional Nile flooding could wet the sand filling the enclosure.
- Schoch's seismic subsurface data may have detected only a **natural bedding plane**, not a weathered region.
- Greater recession of upper strata results from their original 80-degree batter (slope), not preferential rainfall erosion.
- Fissures attributed to **earthquakes and subsurface groundwater** over millions of years.

## The Colin Reader "Middle Position"

Colin Reader (British engineering geologist), published in *Archaeometry* (2001):

- **Concludes the Sphinx enclosure pre-dates the 4th Dynasty** but only by centuries to ~1,000 years — proposing an **Early Dynastic (~3000 BCE)** construction.
- The **western enclosure wall shows the most intense, rounded degradation** (consistent with rainfall runoff), but an area of **ancient quarrying ~20 m west/upslope** (dated to Khufu's reign based on mud seal impressions) would have **intercepted runoff** after it was excavated. Therefore the Sphinx enclosure erosion must predate Khufu's quarrying.
- **Tool-mark preservation argument:** Late Period tombs (c. 664-525 BCE) cut into the western enclosure wall show well-preserved chisel marks. If the intense degradation could occur in "a few generations" (as Lehner & Hawass claim), these 2,500-year-old tool marks should be equally degraded. Their preservation indicates the intense western-wall erosion occurred *before* these tombs were cut.
- The 2nd Dynasty already had large-scale stone quarrying (Gisr el-Mudir at Saqqara), so an Early Dynastic Sphinx is not architecturally implausible.
- Reader's position has gained **some traction at the edges of mainstream Egyptology** but is not consensus.

## Seismic Investigations Beneath the Sphinx

Dobecki & Schoch (1992) found subsurface anomalies ("voids") beneath the Sphinx's paws and flanks — areas of low-velocity seismic waves indicating possible hollow chambers, looser sediment, or natural cavities:
- Evidence of a **cavity/chamber under the left paw**
- A possible tunnel-like feature running the body's length
- Critics (Harrell) argued these reflect natural bedding planes, not artificial voids

## The Giza Complex as Unified Design

The Sphinx is structurally and spatially integrated into Khafre's pyramid complex:

| Element | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Khafre's Causeway | Runs from pyramid to Valley Temple; Sphinx sits north of the lower end |
| Valley Temple | South of Sphinx; connected to causeway |
| Sphinx Temple | Immediately east of Sphinx; built from enclosure blocks; center court nearly identical to Khafre's Mortuary Temple |
| Khafre's Mortuary Temple | Western half's center court nearly identical to Sphinx Temple's — linking them architecturally |

The Sphinx faces due east, aligned to the equinox sunrise, associated with Ra-Horakhty / Harmachis. The layout may reflect a unified astronomical/religious design. See [[shaft-alignments]] and [[purpose-and-function]].

### The Bauval-Hancock Orion Correlation

The three Giza pyramids allegedly match Orion's belt as it appeared in ~10,500 BCE; the Sphinx faces east toward where Leo rose at that epoch. Critiqued by astronomers (Krupp 1997, Fairall 1999, Orofino & Bernardini 2016) on grounds of imprecise correspondence and implausibility of a unified pre-dynastic plan.

### The Inventory Stele

A 26th Dynasty stele (~650 BCE) claims Khufu found the Sphinx already present and built a temple beside it. Mainstream rejection: 2,000 years after the events; classified as a late "pious fraud" with anachronisms.

## Implications for the Wiki

If the Sphinx predates the 4th Dynasty, the entire [[pyramid-evolution-timeline]] may need revision. The Reader position (Early Dynastic, ~3000 BCE) is the most moderate — it doesn't require a "lost civilization" but pushes the Sphinx back by ~500 years, suggesting the Giza Plateau was significant before Khufu. The Schoch position (5000-10000 BCE) would require a complete rethink of Egyptian prehistory. See [[dating-and-chronology]].

The seismic anomalies beneath the Sphinx may connect to the [[tunnels-and-passages]] question — possible subterranean structures beneath the Giza Plateau.
