# Internal Structure

The Great Pyramid contains a complex system of chambers, passages, shafts, and voids that is far more elaborate than any other Egyptian tomb. This internal complexity is the primary evidence that the pyramid's function may extend beyond simple burial.

## Known Chambers

- **Subterranean Chamber**: Cut into bedrock, unfinished. Possibly a plan change, structural problem, or never intended as a burial chamber. The bedrock beneath may contain additional sealed chambers.
- **Queen's Chamber**: Sealed off, may never have been intended for burial. Contains two "air shafts" that do not reach the exterior.
- **King's Chamber**: Contains a granite sarcophagus (too large to have been placed after the chamber was built; no lid; no signs of having held a body). 43 granite ceiling beams weighing 25-80 tonnes each from Aswan.
- **Relieving Chambers**: 4 chambers above the King's Chamber + the superstructure, distributing the 6 million tonnes above. Topmost (Campbell's Chamber) has a gabled roof of massive limestone beams. Contains work-gang graffiti with Khufu's cartouche (1837, Vyse).

## Passages

- **Descending Passage**: Cut through bedrock then continues through masonry at 26°31'23" for 105 m, deviating from straight by less than 1 cm.
- **Ascending Passage**: Connects the Descending Passage to the Grand Gallery and Queen's Chamber.
- **Well Shaft and Grotto**: A vertical shaft connecting the Descending Passage to the Grand Gallery area.

## Grand Gallery

47 m long corbelled vault rising 8.5 m, with an overhanging stepped ceiling. Structurally sophisticated. Possibly designed to relieve load on the King's Chamber and/or serve as a counterweight/mechanical system. See [[grand-gallery-function]].

## Air Shafts

~20 cm² cross-section, cut through up to 70 m of masonry with bends and angle changes. Aligned to specific stellar targets:
- King's Chamber south → Orion's Belt (Alnitak)
- King's Chamber north → Thuban (Polaris predecessor)
- Queen's Chamber south → Sirius
- Queen's Chamber north → Kochab

Calculated by Virginia Trimble (1964), confirmed by Gantenbrink's measurements (1993). See [[shaft-alignments]].

The Upuaut robot (1993, [[gantenbrink]]) found a "door" with copper fittings in the Queen's Chamber southern shaft. A 2002 expedition drilled through and found a small empty space and another blocking stone. Drilling through the second blocking stone has been blocked by bureaucracy for 20+ years.

## Discovered Voids

- **ScanPyramids Big Void** ([[big-void]]): ≥30 m long, above the Grand Gallery, similar cross-section. Published Nature 2017. Shape unresolved (horizontal vs. inclined).
- **North Face Corridor** ([[north-face-corridor]]): Behind chevrons above the original entrance, 17-23 m above ground. Published Nature Communications 2023. Endoscopic inspection confirmed.

## Purpose Question

The consensus treats chambers as a funerary apartment (subterranean → queen's → king's as a progression). But the air shafts, Grand Gallery, relieving chambers, and [[big-void]] suggest a structure whose internal geometry had a purpose beyond mere burial. See [[purpose-and-function]].
