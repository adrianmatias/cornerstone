# Houdin Internal Ramp Hypothesis

Jean-Pierre Houdin proposed that the Great Pyramid contains an internal spiral tunnel within its body, 1-2 m wide, that served as the ramp system for raising blocks during construction.

## What It Solves

- **Volume problem**: No massive external ramp needed (which would require ~1.4 million m³ of material).
- **Casing problem**: The external casing would remain visible and adjustable throughout construction.
- **Geometric control**: The base corners and casing could be continuously surveyed.

## Evidence

- The notch discovered by Houdin at the 3rd course (the "Bob" room) — suggestive but not conclusive.
- Microgravity surveys (2000s) found density variations that could be consistent with an internal ramp but are ambiguous.
- The [[scanpyramids]] muon tomography found the [[big-void]] above the Grand Gallery, not an internal ramp spiral. This does not disprove the theory (the internal ramp would spiral through the pyramid body, not above the Grand Gallery).

## Tests

- Comprehensive muon tomography from outside the pyramid at all four faces, at multiple elevations, with enough angular coverage to detect a 1-2 m tunnel.
- High-resolution microgravity survey using modern gravimeters.
- Both technically feasible but require extensive deployment (months of exposure per position).

## Status

Unproven. If confirmed, the Great Pyramid would be a fundamentally different structure than assumed — the interior would contain an engineered ramp system integrated into the masonry. See [[construction-methods]].
