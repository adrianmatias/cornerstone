# Tunnels and Passages at Giza

Claims of subterranean tunnel systems connecting the Great Pyramid, the Sphinx, and the Nile are among the most persistent and least investigated aspects of the Giza complex. This page distinguishes verified underground features from speculative ones.

## Verified Underground Features

### The Osiris Shaft

The most significant verified underground structure at Giza. Located beneath the causeway of Khafre, south of the Great Pyramid.

- **Three levels**: 
  - First level: a rectangular chamber
  - Second level: six niches carved into the walls
  - Third level: a chamber with a sarcophagus and canopic chest, surrounded by water
- **Discovery**: Recorded by Selim Hassan in the 1930s; excavated by Zahi Hawass starting in 1999; opened to tourists in 2017
- **Dating**: Late Period (Saite, ~664-525 BCE), not Old Kingdom. The current form may reuse older structures.
- **Named after Osiris** because of the ritual objects found within — the third level represents the waters of Osiris
- Herodotus described underground chambers on the hill where the pyramids stand, supplied with water by a channel from the Nile. Hawass interpreted this as a reference to the Osiris Shaft.

### Sphinx Tunnels and Shafts

Multiple verified tunnels and shafts exist beneath and within the Sphinx:
- **Perring's Hole**: Tunnel dug by Perring in the 19th century through the Sphinx's body
- **Rump passage**: A passage at the rear of the Sphinx
- **Keyhole Shaft**: A shaft in the Sphinx enclosure
- **Major fissure**: A natural fissure behind the Sphinx, enlarged and partially cut
- These are mostly 19th-century exploration tunnels or natural features, not ancient constructions

### Subterranean Chamber of the Great Pyramid

Cut into bedrock, unfinished. Possibly a plan change, structural problem, or never intended as a burial chamber. The bedrock beneath may contain additional sealed chambers. See [[internal-structure]].

### The "Grotto" in the Well Shaft

A natural cave intersected by the Well Shaft connecting the Descending Passage to the Grand Gallery area of the Great Pyramid. This is a natural limestone formation, possibly part of a larger cave system. See [[internal-structure]].

### Saite Period Burial Shafts

Various Late Period (c. 664-525 BCE) burial shafts in the Giza necropolis, part of the continued use of the plateau as a cemetery long after the Old Kingdom.

## Geophysical Surveys

### SRI International GPR Survey (1978)
- Ground-penetrating radar survey around the Sphinx and plateau
- Found only **natural fissures** in the bedrock
- No artificial tunnels or chambers detected

### Dobecki Seismography (1991)
- Conducted with [[sphinx-and-complex]] erosion researcher Robert Schoch
- Claimed to detect a **chamber beneath the Sphinx's right paw**
- **Unverified** — no follow-up excavation has confirmed the anomaly
- Critics argued the anomaly reflects a natural bedding plane, not a cavity

### FSU Drilling (1996-98)
- Florida State University conducted drilling programs around the Sphinx
- Found only **natural cavities** in the limestone
- No artificial structures detected

### ScanPyramids ([[scanpyramids]])
- Muon tomography detected the [[big-void]] and [[north-face-corridor]] inside the Great Pyramid
- No subterranean connections detected (muon tomography is optimized for the pyramid's interior, not the subsurface)

## Speculative Features (Unverified)

### The "Hall of Records"

- **Origin**: Edgar Cayce (1877-1945), the "Sleeping Prophet," prophesied a "Hall of Records" beneath the Sphinx's paw, containing the accumulated knowledge of Atlantis. Cayce made this claim in 1933.
- **Status**: Pseudohistory. Nothing has ever been found. The claim was conflated with Schoch's water erosion hypothesis and Bauval's Orion correlation theory in the 1990s.
- The ARE (Association for Research and Enlightenment, Cayce's organization) funded some of the early GPR surveys looking for the Hall of Records.

### Caviglia's Interconnected Passages (1816-19)

- Giovanni Battista Caviglia believed tunnels linked all the Giza pyramids
- Explored the Sphinx enclosure and cleared sand from around the Sphinx
- Vyse repeated these claims in 1837
- **Unsubstantiated** — no connecting tunnels were ever found

### Randall-Stevens Channeled Diagrams (1920s-30s)

- Published diagrams of supposed underground passages at Giza, claimed to have been "channeled" psychically
- **No archaeological support whatsoever**

### Andrew Collins' Cave System (2008)

- Andrew Collins and Nigel Skinner-Simpson reported discovering a cave system beneath the Giza Plateau in 2008
- Based on Henry Salt's journals (British consul-general, early 19th century) describing "caves under the sand"
- Collins identified natural limestone caves, possibly enlarged by human activity
- **Hawass reportedly dismissed the claims**
- The caves are real natural formations but their interpretation as a connecting tunnel system is not supported by mainstream archaeology
- Collins's website and detailed findings were inaccessible during research (returned 404 errors)

### Salt's Caves (1817)

- Henry Salt and Giovanni Caviglia explored a cave system beneath the Giza Plateau in 1817
- Salt's journals describe "caves under the sand" with passages extending deep into the bedrock
- These passages were later lost and their precise location remains uncertain
- May be the same system Collins claimed to rediscover in 2008

## Open Questions

- Do the caves described by Salt and Collins connect to any pyramid structure?
- Is the Grotto in the Great Pyramid's Well Shaft part of a larger natural cave system?
- Could the Subterranean Chamber have been intended to connect to a deeper cave system?
- What does the Dobecki seismic anomaly beneath the Sphinx's paw actually represent?
- Are there subterranean connections between the Sphinx and the pyramids that no survey has been positioned to detect?

The [[malanga-biondi-sar]] claims of 640 m deep shafts beneath Khafre are not a source of evidence here — they have been universally rejected as pseudoscientific (see their page for details).
