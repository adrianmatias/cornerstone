# Malanga-Biondi SAR Claims at the Khafre Pyramid: Research Report

## Executive Summary

In March 2025, Italian researchers Corrado Malanga and Filippo Biondi announced they had discovered vast underground structures — including eight vertical shafts extending ~2,100 feet (648 m) deep, five near-surface chambers, and two massive 80m cubic cavities — beneath the Khafre Pyramid at Giza, using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) Doppler tomography from satellite data. The claims were made via a press conference, not a peer-reviewed publication, and were widely rejected by Egyptologists, radar physicists, and Egyptian antiquities authorities as scientifically implausible and unsubstantiated. Snopes rated the claim "False."

- **Location**: Hotel Castello Artemide Congressi, Bologna, Italy

The group operated under the name **"Khafre Research Project"** (Progetto Khafre / "Progetto Teflon").

### Prior 2022 Publication

- **Paper**: Biondi, F. & Malanga, C. (2022). "Synthetic Aperture Radar Doppler Tomography Reveals Details of Undiscovered High-Resolution Internal Structure of the Great Pyramid of Giza." *Remote Sensing*, 14(20), 5231. DOI: 10.3390/rs14205231
- **Journal**: *Remote Sensing* (MDPI) — an open-access journal. The paper claimed to detect ~20 previously unknown internal structures inside the Great Pyramid (Khufu) using SAR Doppler tomography.

### Key Distinction

The **2022 paper** (on Khufu/Great Pyramid) was published in a peer-reviewed (if low-barrier) journal. The **2025 Khafre claims** were presented **only at a press conference** with a non-peer-reviewed "abstract" PDF. No journal paper for the Khafre findings exists as of this report.

## 2. Methodology: SAR Technology and How It Works

### What SAR Is

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) is an active remote sensing technique where a radar antenna (mounted on a satellite or aircraft) emits microwave pulses toward Earth and records the reflected energy. As the platform moves along its flight path, it synthesizes a large virtual antenna aperture, achieving high-resolution imaging. Key properties:
- **Operates day/night** (doesn't need sunlight)
- **Penetrates clouds** (microwaves pass through)
- **Resolution**: Modern X-band SAR satellites achieve ~0.5–3 meter spatial resolution

### Satellites Used

- **COSMO-SkyMed**: Italian Space Agency constellation, X-band (~9.6 GHz, ~3.1 cm wavelength)
- Also mentioned: **Capella Space** data
- X-band provides fine resolution but has **very limited penetration** into solid materials (centimeters to a few meters at most in dry soil/rock)

### The Biondi-Malanga Innovation: Doppler Tomography via Micro-Vibrations

The team's claimed method works as follows:

1. **Natural seismic noise** (ambient Earth vibrations, micro-seismic background hum) causes **micro-movements** (sub-millimeter to millimeter scale) in both solid rock and any voids/cavities underground.
2. SAR satellites repeatedly image the site. These micro-movements produce detectable **Doppler frequency shifts** (micro-Doppler effects) in the radar returns.
3. Biondi's **proprietary software** converts radar reflections into "phononic" information — treating vibrations like sound waves.
4. Using **multi-chromatic analysis (MCA)**, the radar data is split into many narrow Doppler sub-bands.
5. By tracking how different portions of the structure oscillate differently (solid rock vs. voids have different vibrational modes), the algorithm infers the presence of cavities, shafts, and chambers.
6. Combining multiple satellite passes and viewing angles produces a **3D tomographic model** — claimed to achieve meter-scale resolution down to ~648 m (and potentially ~1,200 m) below surface.

### Why This Is Controversial Physics

- **Standard SAR penetration**: X-band radar (3.1 cm wavelength) scatters within **centimeters to a few meters** in limestone and granite. It cannot directly penetrate hundreds of meters of solid rock.
- The Biondi method **doesn't claim direct penetration** — it claims to detect *surface* micro-vibrations caused by *deeper* phenomena and then computationally infer what's below.
- The critical physics question: **Can surface micro-vibrations uniquely determine deep subsurface structure?** Experts say this is essentially impossible without ground-truth calibration, as the inverse problem is massively underdetermined.
- True tomography (medical CT, seismic surveying) requires waves that **pass through** the medium and are measured from **multiple angles around** the target. SAR from a satellite only provides a **top-down view** — one angle, not the multiple angles needed for genuine tomographic reconstruction.
- The proprietary nature of the processing software means **no independent scientist can verify the algorithm**.

## 3. Scientific Reception and Criticism

### Snopes Rating: FALSE

Snopes published a fact-check on March 21, 2025 (updated March 25), rating the claim **"False"**. Key findings:
- No credible news outlets or scientific publications reported the discovery.
- One of the most widely shared images was **99.9% confirmed AI-generated** (via Hive Moderation analysis).
- The "Khafre Project" has no formal existence beyond Nicole Ciccolo's YouTube channel.
- The claims are an embellishment of already questionable 2022 research, amplified by fringe/conspiracy content creators.

### Expert Criticism

**Lawrence Conyers** (University of Denver, ground-penetrating radar archaeology expert):
- Called the claim of an underground city "a huge exaggeration"
- Stated it is "impossible for the technology to penetrate that deep into the ground"
- Noted he "could not tell if the technology used actually picked up hidden structures below the pyramid"
- Acknowledged that smaller structures (columns, rooms) beneath pyramids are plausible, citing Mesoamerican parallels
- Pointed out the team uses "all sorts of sophisticated proprietary data analysis software" making external verification impossible
- Stated the only way to prove the claims is "targeted excavations"

**Hussein Abdel-Basir** (Egyptologist, former Director General of Giza Pyramids Area, Director of Antiquities Museum at Bibliotheca Alexandrina):
- Called the study lacking "the most basic standards of proper scientific research"
- Stated: "Geophysical techniques such as GPR or seismic analysis can only survey limited depths, not exceeding tens of meters in the best of circumstances. The claim of the existence of huge structures at a depth of 2,000 feet is science fiction."
- Noted Malanga's UFO/alien abduction research background, warning it transforms "scientific research into the promotion of conspiracy theories"
- Emphasized no peer-reviewed paper and no official announcement from Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities or Supreme Council of Antiquities

**Mamdouh al-Damaty** (Former Egyptian Minister of Antiquities):
- Dismissed the claims as "utterly baseless"
- Noted that decades of documented archaeological research have never hinted at secret structures of this scale

### Physics-Based Criticism

- **X-band SAR wavelength** (~3.1 cm) scatters within centimeters in limestone — it physically cannot directly image 648m deep.
- The micro-vibration method is analogous to trying to determine the interior structure of a building by measuring vibrations on its roof — the inverse problem is massively underdetermined.
- **ScanPyramids comparison**: The ScanPyramids project (2015–2023) used **muon tomography** — cosmic ray particles that genuinely penetrate hundreds of meters of solid stone. They found the "Big Void" (~30m long void above the Grand Gallery) published in *Nature* (2017), and a corridor above the entrance (2023). Malanga and Biondi's 2022 claimed internal structures **do not match** the muon-verified findings — if SAR truly "saw" inside Khufu, the muon-confirmed Big Void should appear in their data. It doesn't.
- **Robert Schoch** (geologist, Boston University, known for challenging mainstream Egyptology on Sphinx dating): Called the Khafre claims "a bridge too far" and criticized the methodology.
- **Christopher Dunn** (author of *The Giza Power Plant*, fringe theorist): Even he distanced himself, calling the SAR findings "speculative overreach."

### Concerns About AI-Generated Images

- The 3D renderings shared widely on social media were so polished that multiple observers suspected AI generation.
- Snopes confirmed one of the most-shared images was **99.9% likely AI-generated** per Hive Moderation.
- The actual raw SAR data and processing pipeline have not been made publicly available.

## 6. Broader Context: GPR and SAR in Archaeology

### Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR)

- **How it works**: A ground-based antenna sends short electromagnetic pulses into the earth. Reflections from boundaries between materials of different dielectric properties (soil/stone, soil/air, soil/water) return to the receiver, creating vertical profiles.
- **Resolution**: Centimeters to tens of centimeters, depending on antenna frequency.
- **Penetration depth**: 
  - Optimal conditions (dry sand): up to ~10–20 meters with low-frequency antennas (100 MHz)
  - Typical conditions: ~4–6 meters
  - **Cannot penetrate hundreds of meters** — this is a fundamental physics limitation, not a technology limitation
- **Limitations**: Fails in wet clay or saline soils (signal absorbed); requires ground access; interpretation requires expertise (false positives common)

### SAR in Archaeology

- **Standard applications**: Surface mapping, detecting subsurface features in **dry sandy environments** (a few meters deep at most with low-frequency bands), monitoring looting, finding buried riverbeds
- **Notable successes**: 
  - NASA Shuttle Imaging Radar found ancient trade routes and the lost city of Ubar in Oman (through dry sand)
  - SAR mapping of Angkor's waterways under forest canopy
  - Sarah Parcak's satellite remote sensing for identifying archaeological sites across the Mediterranean
- **Penetration**: Low-frequency SAR (P-band, L-band) can penetrate a few meters into dry sand/soil. **X-band (used by Biondi) penetrates only centimeters into rock.**
- **Standard use is surface or near-surface** — not deep subsurface tomography through solid rock.

### Comparison: Muon Tomography (ScanPyramids)

The most validated non-invasive method for pyramid interior imaging:
- **How it works**: Cosmic ray muons (subatomic particles) naturally penetrate the atmosphere and pass through solid stone. Detectors measure muon flux — denser areas absorb more muons, voids allow more through.
- **Penetration**: Hundreds of meters of solid stone — genuinely works at the depths relevant to pyramid interiors.
- **Validation**: ScanPyramids' 2017 "Big Void" discovery was published in *Nature* and independently confirmed by multiple international teams using different detector technologies. The 2023 corridor discovery above Khufu's entrance was confirmed with endoscopic probes.
- **Contrast**: Malanga and Biondi's SAR method has achieved no such validation. Their 2022 claimed structures inside Khufu do not match the muon-verified voids.

### Other Technologies

- **Electrical resistivity**: Used in the 1970s–1990s at Giza (SRI surveys around Sphinx); limited depth, ambiguous results
- **Seismic refraction**: Used in 1991 around the Sphinx (Schoch/Dobecki); detected possible shallow cavity (~7-8m deep); follow-up drilling found nothing significant
- **Japanese Waseda University GPR** (1980s): Found some anomalies at Giza; subsequent excavation identified them as natural fissures or known small tombs
- **L-shaped anomaly** (2024): Japanese-Egyptian team using GPR found a shallow (~6.5 ft deep) L-shaped anomaly near Khufu — modest, verifiable, published in a peer-reviewed journal

### The Pattern

The history of geophysical investigation at Giza shows:
1. Technology occasionally detects anomalies
2. Anomalies generate excitement and speculation
3. Ground-truth investigation usually reveals natural features or much smaller structures than initially hypothesized
4. No large underground complex has ever been verified despite over a century of investigation
5. The Malanga-Biondi claims are **vastly larger in scale** than any previously detected anomaly, making them even more extraordinary — and requiring even more evidence that has not been provided.
