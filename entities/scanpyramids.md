# ScanPyramids

International project (launched October 2015) led by Cairo University and the French HIP Institute (Heritage Innovation Preservation). Aims to scan Old Kingdom pyramids (Khufu, Khafre, Bent, Red) using non-invasive, non-destructive techniques: infrared thermography, muon tomography, 3D simulation and reconstruction.

## Discoveries

### ScanPyramids Big Void (SP-BV) — [[big-void]]
- Announced November 2, 2017, published in Nature.
- ≥30 m long, similar cross-section to the Grand Gallery, above the Grand Gallery.
- Observed by three independent teams: Nagoya University (nuclear emulsion plates), KEK (scintillator hodoscope), CEA (gas detectors) — all from different viewpoints in the Queen's Chamber and outside the North Face.
- Shape (horizontal vs. inclined) remains unresolved due to limited angular resolution of muon tomography.

### North Face Corridor (SP-NFC) — [[north-face-corridor]]
- Published Nature Communications, March 2023.
- 17-23 m above ground, 0.7-2 m from the north face, corridor-shaped.
- Endoscopic inspection confirmed it is a real void.
- Located behind the chevrons above the original entrance.

## Reactions

- Zahi Hawass initially dismissed: "They found nothing. This paper offers nothing to Egyptology. Zero." Later called the NFC a "major discovery."
- Egypt's Ministry of Antiquities initially said voids were known, then acknowledged the discovery.
- Physicists confirmed robustness: three independent experiments with different techniques.
