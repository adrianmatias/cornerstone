# Diary of Merer (Wadi al-Jarf Papyri / Red Sea Scrolls)

The only surviving contemporary textual record of pyramid construction. Papyrus logbooks written over 4,500 years ago by Merer, a mid-ranking inspector (sehedj). Discovered 2013 at Wadi al-Jarf by Pierre Tallet's French mission. Dated to year 27 of [[khufu]]'s reign (~2589-2566 BC). Called "the greatest discovery in Egypt in the 21st century" by Zahi Hawass.

## Contents

The best-preserved sections (Papyrus Jarf A and B) document:
- Transport of white limestone blocks from Tura North and Tura South to Giza
- About 40 boatmen under Merer's command
- Every ~10 days: 2-3 round trips, shipping ~30 blocks of 2-3 tonnes each → ~200 blocks/month
- 4-day round trip: Tura → She-Khufu (harbour) → Akhet-Khufu → back to Tura
- Period covered: July to November (inundation season)
- Ankhhaf (Khufu's half-brother and vizier) oversaw the harbour operations

## Critical Significance

1. Confirms stones came by boat via a Nile branch that reached the plateau (the "Ahramat Branch," confirmed 2024 by satellite imaging and sediment cores — see [[nile-branch]])
2. The workforce was organized, salaried, and bureaucratized — not slave labor
3. Casing was being applied near the end of Khufu's reign (year 27), confirming casing as the final construction phase
4. The papyri cover ONLY the Tura casing stone deliveries — no record of core construction, granite transport, or internal chamber building

## Other Papyri

- Papyrus Jarf C: building a "double djadja" in the central Delta
- Papyrus Jarf D: work for the Residence and the Valley Temple (?) of Khufu
- Other logbooks (E-L): fragmentary, contents yet to be fully published
