# Malanga-Biondi SAR Findings (Khafre Pyramid)

Corrado Malanga (University of Pisa) and Filippo Biondi (University of Strathclyde) claimed to have detected eight massive vertical shafts extending over 2,100 feet (640 m) beneath the Khafre Pyramid at Giza, using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR). **These claims have been universally rejected by both the scientific and Egyptological communities.**

## The Claim

Announced at a **4-hour press conference on March 15, 2025** in Bologna, Italy, by Malanga, Biondi, and Armando Mei (the "Khafre Research Project"). They claimed:
- Eight vertical shafts descending ~2,100 feet (640 m) below the Khafre Pyramid
- Additional structures including chambers, passages, and a vast underground complex
- A subterranean system connecting structures beneath the Giza Plateau

**No peer-reviewed paper was published** — only a "Project Abstract" PDF. The team had a prior 2022 paper in *Remote Sensing* (MDPI) on the Great Pyramid (Khufu), but that paper's findings do not match verified muon tomography results from [[scanpyramids]].

## Methodology — and Why It Doesn't Work

The team used **X-band SAR data from COSMO-SkyMed satellites**. Their claimed innovation is "Doppler tomography" — detecting surface micro-vibrations caused by ambient seismic noise, then using proprietary software to convert these into 3D subsurface images.

**Critical physics problems:**
1. **X-band radar penetrates only centimeters into limestone.** It cannot image 640 m beneath a pyramid.
2. **Genuine tomography requires multiple viewing angles**, which a single-orbit satellite cannot provide.
3. **The algorithm is proprietary and unverifiable** — no raw data has been released.
4. The most-shared image purporting to show the underground complex was confirmed to be **99.9% AI-generated**.

## Scientific Reception: Universally Negative

- **Snopes** rated the claim **"False."**
- **Lawrence Conyers** (University of Denver, GPR expert): called it "a huge exaggeration" and said the technology cannot penetrate that deep.
- Even **fringe theorists** (Christopher Dunn, Robert Schoch) distanced themselves from the claims.
- The 2022 MDPI paper passed that journal's (low-bar) review but has **never been independently validated** and contradicts ScanPyramids' muon-verified findings.

## Egyptological Response: Categorical Rejection

- **Zahi Hawass**: "completely wrong, fake news... will go to the dustbin of history." Withdrew from a conference rather than share a platform with the team.
- **Mamdouh al-Damaty** (former Minister of Antiquities): "utterly baseless."
- **Hussein Abdel-Basir** (Egyptologist): "science fiction."
- **No excavation permits were issued.**

## Broader Context: GPR and SAR in Archaeology

- **GPR (Ground Penetrating Radar)** typically penetrates only tens of meters into rock.
- **SAR X-band** penetrates centimeters into rock — useful for surface deformation monitoring, not deep subsurface imaging.
- The only validated deep-penetration method for pyramid interiors is **muon tomography** ([[scanpyramids]], published in *Nature*).
- Historical geophysical surveys at Giza (1970s-2020s) have found only small anomalies, all resolved as natural features or modest structures — never anything approaching kilometer-scale underground complexes.

## Assessment

The Malanga-Biondi claims are **pseudoscientific** — they use the language and instruments of science (SAR satellites, "tomography") but violate fundamental physics (penetration depth), lack peer review, refuse to release raw data, and have been rejected by every credentialed expert who has examined them. The AI-generated visualization images further undermine credibility.

This does NOT mean there are no subterranean structures beneath Giza — the [[tunnels-and-passages]] page documents verified underground features (Osiris Shaft, Collins caves, seismic anomalies). But the Malanga-Biondi claims provide no credible evidence for any of them.
