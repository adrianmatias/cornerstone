# Khufu (Cheops)

Pharaoh of the Fourth Dynasty, Old Kingdom Egypt. Attributed as the builder of the Great Pyramid (Akhet Khufu, "Horizon of Khufu"). Estimated reign: ~2589-2566 BC (estimates vary from 2613-2577 BC per Ramsey et al. 2010 to 2480 BC per Spence 2000). Son of Snefru.

## Attribution Evidence

- Work-gang graffiti in the relieving chambers above the King's Chamber (discovered 1837 by Vyse), bearing Khufu's cartouche over a dozen times.
- The [[diary-of-merer]] (2013 discovery) independently confirms the name "Akhet Khufu" for the pyramid.
- The surrounding cemetery contains tombs of Khufu's family and officials.
- Mortuary cult operated in the complex temples for centuries.

## Controversies

- The Vyse relieving chamber inscriptions were found under less-than-ideal archaeological conditions. Some researchers have questioned their authenticity, though the convergence of evidence (including the Diary of Merer) strongly supports authenticity. See [[dating-and-chronology]].
- No mummy has ever been found in the Great Pyramid. The sarcophagus shows no lid and no signs of having held a body. See [[purpose-and-function]].
- The "26 years" construction duration is derived from the estimated reign length, not from a direct measurement. The Diary of Merer dates to year 27, suggesting a reign of at least 27 years.

## Family

- Father: Snefru (built three pyramids: Meidum, Bent, Red — see [[pyramid-evolution-timeline]])
- Half-brother: Ankhhaf (vizier, overseer of harbour operations per the Diary of Merer)
- Son: Djedefre (successor; his cartouches on boat pit sealing blocks indicate he completed the burial)
- Vizier: Hemiunu (presumed architect of the Great Pyramid)
